## 1. Camera near rock: lifts, pulls in and jitters; rock should fade instead

**Built 09:25 (world-look):** see the board, "## Morning builds" › world-look (check: `tools/check_rock_fade.py`).

**What the user saw:** with the camera close to rocks or a mountain, the camera "zooms up or jitters". The view fills
with rock facets and the unit ends up at the edge (screenshot, 08:2x: Thorimbek beside a ridge at night). The user's
idea: fade the rock instead.

**Why it happens** (`game/Interface/remastered.html`, render loop, lines ~4150–4166). Three mechanisms move the
camera away from rock, and none of them fades anything:

1. **The aim height averages 5×5 tiles of ground round the focus, rock tops included.** `gA` sums `v3groundAt` over
   25 tiles; a mountain tile counts at its full top height (6.4 on a big ridge). Standing next to a cliff raises the
   aim point (`aimH = gA*0.6 + low`, eased at 2.5/s), so the whole camera floats up. Stepping away drops it again.
   This is the "zooms up".
2. **The eye floor snaps up over rock.** The eye must stay 0.7 above the ground under it (`V3.eyeG`). It jumps up at
   once when the ground under the eye is 0.3 higher, then eases down (12/s up, 4/s down). Turning or panning moves
   the eye across rock and grass tiles, so it pops up and sinks back: jitter.
3. **The boom pulls in at once and eases out slowly.** A ray from the focus to the eye, in 6 % steps; rock met after
   a clear stretch pulls the camera in front of it instantly (`V3.boom`), easing back out at 1.5/s. The steps make
   the pull-in jump between positions, and instant-in/slow-out pumps as you move. In iso the near plane also scales
   with the boom.
4. A last floor keeps the eye 0.4 above the ground under it.

**What already fades today:**
- Trees only. The shader dithers geometry marked with vertex alpha 0.955–0.975 (trees use 0.97) over up to 8 of
  your units and 4 stations (the `fp` points, lines ~4186–4191), and close to the eye (`fnear`). Shader at line
  ~2871.
- Roofs are see-through while playing.
- The mountain cut (`V3.mcR`, `v3mcutTile`, lines ~4084–4119) trenches the rock only when the focus is *inside*
  a mountain (halls, tunnels). A camera beside a mountain gets the three moves above instead.

**Plan (to build later, with the user's go):**
1. **Stop moving the camera for rock.**
   - Aim at the ground the focus actually stands on: the focus tile's walkable height, or the locked or selected
     unit's ground, and skip rock tops in the average. Keep easing for real level changes (a ramp onto a
     mountain top).
   - Drop the boom pull-in for rock.
   - Keep only a soft floor for the eye, eased the same up and down with no snap, so it never ends inside the
     rock. Or allow it inside and rely on the near cut below.
2. **Fade the rock that is in the way, Diablo-style:**
   - Mark rock geometry for the shader the way trees are marked: its own alpha band, or a per-draw uniform for the
     rock chunks.
   - Dither away rock fragments that lie between the eye and the focus inside a soft-edged cylinder (radius ~2
     tiles), and in front of your units (the existing `fp` points).
   - Cut everything within ~1 tile of the eye (`fnear` for rock).
   - Leave a faint rim, or keep the faded rock's silhouette, so the mountain's shape still reads.
   - No darkening (memory sun-not-camera-shadow); your own units are never hidden.
3. **Clicks go through faded rock:** picking (`v3pickTile`, the hover verdict) skips rock that is faded at that
   pixel, so the unit or ground behind it can be clicked. In the screenshot the tag read "Nothing to do there".
4. **Keep the mountain cut** for halls inside mountains as it is.
5. **Checks:**
   - A headless probe orbiting a unit beside a ridge: eye height and distance per frame show no jumps, the aim
     height stays flat, before/after stills, the unit visible in every frame.
   - The real page in WKWebView.
   - A short real-app check through the lock.
   - Frame time unchanged.

**Owner:** world-look (3D drawing, shader) with rts-feel (camera input). This touches the shared render loop in
`remastered.html`.

**For the user to decide when it's built:**
- How faded rock looks: a dither see-through like trees, or a hard cut down to a fixed height like the halls.
- How much fades: only between the camera and the focus and your units, or everything within a few tiles of the
  camera.

## 5. "Remove the sky limit and join it with the surface, so the full mountain can be mined?"

**Short answer: no need, and it would be a big rewrite.** The sky layer isn't what stops mining.

- **The rock is all on the surface layer.** Each rock tile there is a column of 3–8 blocks (`Breaking.dm`). From
  outside, a miner reaches the bottom two (soon the player's pick of those two, section 2). The blocks above are the
  tunnel's roof and are already mined from inside, one block an order (`Mountains.dm`). So the whole mountain can
  already be dug out, block by block.
- **The sky layer is where units walk on top of things:**
  - the walkable mountain tops ("Peak" tiles above the rock, `Peaks.dm`);
  - wall walks (`WallWalks.dm`);
  - the upper storey of buildings (`Stairs.dm`, `Building.dm`);
  - lifts up a cliff (`Lifts.dm`);
  - carved steps up a mountain (`Mountains.dm`);
  - flyers (`Flying.dm`).
- **Joining the layers** would mean units standing at different heights on one layer. The original movement has one
  walkable tile per layer and no heights, so it would be a rewrite of movement, pathing, saves and the 3D view, with
  a big risk of breaking the original game's rules.

**If the wish is to dig a mountain from its top down** (stand on the peak and mine downward), that fits the current
layers: a Mine order from a peak would take the top block of the column under it, and once the column is gone, the
peak opens to the sky (as `Peaks.dm` already does). To plan only if the user wants it.

**User's decision (08:4x):** "we will keep sky layer its own thing and treat it like depths": the sky layer stays a
separate level of its own, like the depths, and is not merged into the surface.
The user, a moment later: "a entrance or a thing that can push them to the sky levels". So the sky gets its own
ways in, the way the depths have Hellgates (`obj/Portal`, `PermPortal1/2`, View3D.dm `hellgate`) and caves have
cave mouths (`RemasterCaveMouth`): an entrance or a force that carries units up to the sky level (for example a
sky gate or an updraft). Today the only ways up are stairs, lifts, carved steps up a mountain, and flying. To plan
when the user picks this up: what the entrance is, where it appears, whether it goes both ways, and what waits up there.

## 6. A lost limb needs treating: cauterize with a torch, or bandage it

**The user's idea (09:2x):** "if a leg is missing the user would need a torch to cauterize the wound or bandages to
stop that missing limb from bleeding out".

**Today:**
- Losing a limb costs 90 blood (`Combats.dm`, then `BloodLoss()` sets `BleedType` by the blood left: Very Mildly
  ≤300, Mildly ≤260, Badly ≤130, Very Badly ≤90, Extremly ≤20).
- `Bleeding()` (`Health.dm`) drops blood every 5 s. A death chance applies only at Badly or worse.
- There's a **20 % chance every 5 s that bleeding stops on its own, and the blood then refills to full** (original
  behaviour).
- The only treatment is the Athelas poultice from the Doctor's stall (`Doctor.dm`), which the Doctor job uses at
  Badly or worse.
- There's no torch cauterizing and no bandage item; "bandaged" after surgery is only a look (`Speech.dm`).

**Proposed:**
- **A stump bleeds until treated.** No stopping on its own; the blood drains steadily (faster for a leg or arm,
  slower for a hand).
- **Three ways to stop it:**
  - **Cauterize with fire:** a lit torch carried, or a campfire or forge beside the unit. Fast and sure, but it hurts
    (HP and mood), and the burn can fester.
  - **Bandage:** a new item made from cloth, leather or spider silk (which workshop makes it is to decide). It slows
    the bleeding at once and stops it after a short while; poor bandages may need redoing.
  - **The Athelas poultice:** stops the bleeding and heals, as now.
- **Who does it:** the unit itself if it can (not with both arms gone), a nearby villager, or the Doctor job, all
  fitting into Care.dm's self-care and the Doctor's cases. The unit card's Health tab shows "Stump, bleeding:
  needs fire or a bandage" with buttons.
- **Blood comes back slowly** after the bleeding stops, not all at once.
- Other wounds keep the original's bleeding unless the user wants the same rule for them.

**Where:** `Health.dm` `Bleeding()` (layered with `..()` from Remastered code), `Combats.dm` limb loss →
`BloodLoss()`, `Doctor.dm` cases and remedies, `Care.dm`, a bandage item and recipe, the Health tab.

**The user's rules for it (09:3x):** "bandages [reduce] bleeding a lot but the user still bleeds, and cauterize stops
bleeding, and the potions etc. [and] fire spells can work as well". So:
- **Bandage:** cuts the bleeding a lot but doesn't stop it; the unit still loses blood slowly until it's cauterized
  or healed.
- **Cauterize:** stops the bleeding. Any fire does it: a carried torch, a campfire or forge beside the unit, or a
  fire spell cast on the wound by a caster of yours (it hurts, as fire does).
- **Potions and remedies work too:** the Athelas poultice stops it and heals. The original Potion of Restoration
  (`HealPotion`, Turfs.dm) "will heal any lost limbs", so it regrows the limb, which ends the stump altogether.
  Other healing brews (Brewing.dm) can be checked for the same.

**How health comes back today** (for the plan):
- **Natural healing:** two original loops, each about +10 HP every 40 s: `Heal()` in Health.dm and the
  `Hunger()` loop in Mob Procs.dm. So about 20 HP per 40 s; a 350-HP unit takes around 10 minutes from near death.
  Inner organs mend +1.8 (brain, heart, throat +1) each time. Arms and legs don't mend that way.
- **The Athelas poultice:** +80 HP and stops bleeding (Doctor.dm).
- **A weapon's drain enchantment:** a share of the damage dealt comes back as HP (WeaponSkills.dm).
- **Coming back after being away:** survivors of poison and sickness are healed to full, having "rested it off"
  (Away.dm, FoodSupply.dm).
- **Full heal:** for new units and the admin command (`FullHeal`).
- **Not there:** sleeping in a bed doesn't heal faster (beds only restore tiredness), and there's no healing spell
  in the code.

## 7. Melee armour as armor / (armor + k)

**The user (13:5x):** "switch melee armour to armor / (armor + k): pick k from the game's real armour values, show
before/after damage tables, then build it."

**Before:** every melee blow lost a flat Defence / 2: `Combats.dm` ~376 for ordinary blows, `WeaponSkills.dm`
`RmMoveBlow` for weapon-skill moves. It was floored at 5 damage. A mace counted half the armour, and a flying
gargoyle's armour counted half.

**Real numbers** (measured in the arena; every playable race starts with 350 HP):
- Bare Defence: 0 (Ratman, Demon) to 19 (Frogman); 38–40 for Vampire and Gargoyle.
- A 6-piece metal set adds 60 (Poor) / 72 (Quality) / 90 (Masterful) / 102 (Legendary). Adamantium is ~111.
- A metal weapon averages 17–28 by quality (adamantium 35).
- Strength is ~1–24 at level 1 and ~72–130 once levelled. Strength / 2 adds to the blow.
- Recipes add `Defence += 25` and then `MetalArmourCraft()` overwrites it, so that +25 never counts (original quirk,
  left alone).

**k = 100:** 100 armour halves a blow. A bare human (7) takes 7 % off, a full Quality set (79) 44 %, a Legendary set
(109) 52 %, and the toughest kit (142) 59 %. It never reaches 100 %. (k = 80 gives 50 % for a Quality set; k = 120
gives 40 %.)

### 7c. Per body part (user, 14:3x: "make it per body part: a blow that hits the head is reduced by the helmet's armour, a leg hit by the leggings")

The share now comes from the armour covering the part that's hit, not the whole Defence (`RemasterPartArmour`,
CombatSkills.dm).
- **Which piece covers what:** as the original's deflect check pairs them.
  - Head: helmet (and a crown).
  - Upper body, lower body and wings: chestplate.
  - Left and right arm: each arm's own piece.
  - Both legs: leggings.
  - The shield covers no part; it still blocks whole blows.
- **The part's armour** = the unit's own toughness (its Defence with nothing on: 0–19 for most races, about 40 for a
  gargoyle or vampire) + **6 × the covering piece's Defence**. A full suit is six pieces, so a part under a Quality
  piece (12) is exactly as protected as the whole body used to be in a full Quality suit. The 7–7b tables still hold,
  per part. An uncovered part has only the unit's own toughness. A creature that wears nothing has its hide
  everywhere.
- **Where each attack lands:**
  - Melee: each region the blow lands on loses that region's share (`Combats.dm`).
  - Arrows: the spot they hit (legs, arms, eyes, heart, lungs, spleen; any other roll counts as the body).
  - Thrown weapons: where they land (a leg, an arm or the chest).
  - Weapon-skill moves: the body.
- Checked in the arena (11/11) and with check_combat, check_weaponskills, check_combat_skills, check_throwing,
  check_bow_controller and check_projectile_defense.

## 8. Fog of war from your units' sight (plan; the user, 15:5x: "plan a fog of war")

**Today:**
- On the surface and the sky level everything in the 3D area counts as in sight (`View3D.dm`: `open` → every tile
  "1"). Enemies anywhere near the camera are sent and drawn (`Remaster3DThings` uses `range`, which ignores walls).
- Underground there's real line of sight from the camera's view: rock hides, seen-before tiles are dim, never-seen
  tiles are dark. The page already draws those states (`v3meshes` "touch.dark"; `v3visible` hides others' units
  off "1" tiles).
- `Explore.dm` remembers what the player has seen: 5 tiles round each unit and 12 round the camera, saved per level.
  The minimap and world map show explored land only.

**Proposed: StarCraft-style, from units, never the camera.** This keeps the earlier decision: no darkness that
follows the camera, and your own units are never hidden (memory sun-not-camera-shadow).
- **Three states for each tile:**
  - Never seen: dark, as underground.
  - Explored but not watched now: dimmed and slightly grey. The land is as you last saw it, with no live creatures.
  - In sight now: full colour, everything live.
- **Who gives sight:**
  - Each of your units, **12 tiles** by line of sight (`view()`: rock and walls block). The user chose it (16:2x)
    after a preview (`reports/polish-2026-10-22/coordinator/fog-preview-sheet.jpg`). At the default Iso camera, 10
    already filled most of the screen; zoomed out it felt tight. 12 clears the longest bow (10 then; 8 now) with room to spare.
  - On a mountain top, wall walk or tower: +3. Flying: +3. At night: −3, except races that see in the dark.
    Blind units: none.
  - Your workshops and buildings: about 4.
  - Allies share their sight (`RemasterAllied`).
- **What's hidden:**
  - Other players' units, NPCs and animals outside sight are **not sent** (server side): no peeking through the page,
    and less to send.
  - Buildings and workshops you've seen stay drawn grey where you last saw them, as a remembered picture, until you
    see them again.
  - The far view keeps drawing explored land only.
- **The minimap** uses the same three states: dark checker for never seen, dim for explored, bright for in sight.
  Enemy dots only show in sight.
- **Orders:** move and attack-move into fog work as now. A unit you can't see can't be clicked. An attack order on
  a target that walks out of sight goes on to its last seen spot, then stops.
- **Look (user's choice, 16:2x):** soft edges where sight ends (shaded per corner, never stair-step squares),
  **a dim blue-grey for never-seen land, not black**, explored land only lightly greyed, and a gentle fade when a
  tile comes into sight. The preview showed today's underground style (black, hard tile steps) reads as cramped.
  It should feel like Warcraft III / StarCraft II.
- **Speed:**
  - Sight is worked out per player from units that moved since the last tick (a cache per unit and tile), on the
    3D tick.
  - It's sent as the same `seen` string the underground already uses.
  - Measured against the 200-unit stress world.

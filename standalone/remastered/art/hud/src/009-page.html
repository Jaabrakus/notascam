A place to call home

Start small. Keep your company fed, equipped and warm, then grow a settlement that can defend itself.

How to play

  • Select one of your people by clicking them (or their row in Company); drag a box for several.
  • Give orders by clicking: something on the ground to pick up, open ground to walk; with Gather on (G, in the bar at the bottom), a tree to chop, rock to mine (any block up its face: the miner works up to it) or a fishing spot. With it off, right-click a tree or rock to chop or mine. Up on a mountain, Gather on digs its top down. With several selected, a tree or rock goes to one of them and the ground moves them all.
  • Do now replaces what a unit is doing; Add tasks (or Shift+click) adds to its list. Work carries on when you deselect or pick someone else.
  • Stores: set a stockpile in the Work tab. Drop off and Unload & resume take carried goods there; Auto unload on the unit card does it by itself.
  • Pause, Resume and Stop are on the unit card. Stop clears its orders and pauses its job and crafting.
  • Workshops: with a unit selected, click one to open its menu (Craft, and more); the nearest of them walks over first.
  • Right-click anything to see what you can do with it. The unit card’s tabs hold its bag, health, family, combat, skills and spells; click a bag item to use it.
  • If something can’t be done, the unit card says why (no tool, too heavy, can’t reach) and Resume tries again.

More to know

  • Bag bar on the unit card: click an item for its choices, double-click to use it, drag it onto another unit to give it. Unload / Take buttons work with the stores; Best armour puts on the best pieces carried.
  • Groups: with several units selected, pick a formation (Keep, Line, Column, Box) and a stance. Patrol › Set route, click points, Finish.
  • Village tab: Roster, Lineage, Bulletin board (work orders, workshop orders with costs) and Log (click an entry to go there; Messages keeps every message and fight line of the session).
  • Army tab: Formations (the shape a group keeps when you send it) and Captives (who you hold, their rope and loyalty).
  • Work tab: stockpile and stockpile zones (food store, ore yard, armoury...).
  • Place tab: pick a wall, floor, bridge, stairs or workshop and paint it on the ground. Each plan is a construction task that shows what it needs ("10 stone: 8 in stockpiles, 2 missing"). Assign workers there, or give villagers the Builder job: they fetch the material from the stockpiles, build tile by tile and take leftovers back. With nothing in stock the task waits and posts a work order on the board.
  • Map (top right): everything you have explored. Pings on the minimap show recent fights, births and deaths.
  • Camera: right-drag moves it; F9 (or Chase, under the minimap) follows the selected unit. 3D: middle-drag turns it and tilts it down to the horizon (Q/E turn too); click, double-click, right-click menu, drag a box; N and Top camera buttons. Ctrl+Z or Undo removes the last plan or order.
  • Hover a target with units selected to see what they would do and who can't. Each morning a short report sums up the day.

Shortcuts

  • Select: click a unit; drag a box; Shift+click adds or removes; double-click (or Ctrl/Cmd+click) one of yours selects all of that people on screen; Ctrl+A selects everyone. Shift+Space or Escape deselects (work goes on); with a window open, Escape closes it first.
  • Orders: left-click a target to give an order; Shift+click queues it after the current work. A, then a click: attack, or attack-move on open ground (Esc or a right-click cancels). P stops. F aims a race special. G turns Gather on or off; B opens the Build bar (B or Esc goes back). Ctrl+Z undoes the last plan or order. Right-click opens the menu of choices, and a second right-click on the same spot runs its main one (Settings can make right-click give orders instead, as in StarCraft).
  • Groups: Ctrl+1–9 keeps the selection as a group, Shift+1–9 adds to it, 1–9 calls it back (press twice: the camera goes there). The chips above the action bar do the same.
  • Camera: arrow keys or the window edge pan, the wheel zooms, Q/E turn, N faces north, Z centres on the selection, C cuts the rock away (or shows this layer only), X shows the layer above as a ghost; right-drag moves, middle-drag turns. Press or drag on the minimap to go there; right-click it to send the selected units there.
  • Finding people: F1 selects the next idle unit; Backspace goes to the unit under attack.
  • Time: Space pauses and resumes; give orders while paused. F3 opens this guide.

Seasons & survival

Fullness and rest are remaining reserves: low values need attention. Cold and blood loss are warnings. Campfires provide warmth; food, beds and equipment matter. Different races use different tools and recipes.

Alchemy & the Cauldron

Brew potions in a bottle at a campfire, or in big batches in a Cauldron. Drink them, or throw them to burst into a cloud: healing mist, sleeping mist, poison, smoke, or fire.

  1. Build a Cauldron: Place › Cauldron (1 stone, one tile). A builder walks over and builds it.
  2. Stand one unit beside it and select only that unit: the Cauldron box appears on its unit card.
  3. Fill it: Add puts in one ingredient from the bag (5 units), ×N adds all of that kind. Pour water adds 30 if a lake or river is within 6 tiles. It holds 150.
  4. Heat it: Add wood (each piece burns about a minute); it heats up to 160°. Douse / Light. A brew forms on its own once the mix is right and hot enough.
  5. Bottle it: carry Empty Bottles and press Fill bottles (up to 30 each). The unit's Alchemy box can then Drink or Throw (click a tile up to 7 away).

No Cauldron? A unit with an Empty Bottle can brew 30 units at a time: its Alchemy box adds ingredients, draws water beside a lake, and heats the bottle at a campfire within 2 tiles. The ★ recipes need the Cauldron.

Rune circuits: doors, traps and alarms

Rune pieces carry mana like wires carry power. Pieces touching side by side form one circuit; its Mana crystals pay for everything on it. A pressure plate or lever sends a signal, and every device on the circuit acts at once.

  1. Build the pieces from the workshop picker (Work › Place a workshop, or the 3D ghost). Rune lines stay in place mode: click tile after tile, Esc when done. A builder walks to each tile and builds it.
  2. Power it: put a Mana crystal (1 gem) on the circuit. Crystals hold 100 mana and recharge slowly on their own; mana vapour (thrown Mana Elixir) on a crystal charges it fast.
  3. Wire it: lay rune lines so the plate or lever, the crystal and the devices all touch side by side. Diagonals don't connect.
  4. Use it: select one unit beside a piece; the Runes box shows the circuit's mana and lets you Pull a lever, Open/Close a door, Test a signal, set a plate to go off for Enemies only or Anyone, or Scratch out a piece (cutting the circuit there).

War, sieges and peace

Realms start at peace: your people won't attack another realm's. In the Realm tab, a ruler can Declare war (fighting starts after 60 seconds, or at once if they attacked you first) and Offer peace; the other ruler accepts or refuses, and peace brings a 10-minute truce. A red strip at the top shows each war and its score (1 a kill, 5 a banner, 1 a capture).

Sieges: stand your people within 2 tiles of an enemy banner with nobody guarding it within 5: a ring and a bar fill up, and at 100% the banner and its land are yours, with a quarter of their coins. A realm that loses its last banner sues for peace. At war you may also dig and tear down inside their land.

Siege engines

Build them at the Carpentry: a Battering Ram (3 wood) pounds a wall or door beside it; a Catapult (4 wood, 1 stone) hurls boulders 3 to 10 tiles and hurts anyone where they land. An engine moves and fires only with one of your people within 2 tiles. Click it to select it, then click a wall or door (or Attack and click one, or an enemy for the catapult). Walls: wood 1,000, stone 3,000, obsidian 4,500; doors 10. A Ballista (3 wood, 1 lump of iron) shoots a heavy bolt straight at an enemy up to 12 tiles off: built for great beasts, it hits the part it is aimed at and, at a dragon in the air, mostly its wings.

Leave an engine without crew and enemies at war (or raiders) beside it will wreck it and may set it alight; your crew beat out the flames. Engines are saved with the world.

Families, captives and your house

Have a child: select one unit; the unit card lists partners nearby. Choose one and the pair go off together; the card then shows "Expecting".

Capture: subdue an enemy you may fight (stun, web or knock it out, or wound it badly), then use Capture on the unit card or the right-click list with one of your people beside it. Tying a person takes a rope your people carry (or one lying on a stockpile, which they fetch): plant rope from wood at a Carpentry, leather or silk rope at a Leatherworks, or Iron Chains from a Forge; better rope is harder to break. A captive never joins by itself: on its card's Interact tab, Win over (one of yours talks and shares food until loyalty reaches 100%) or Enslave (works for you, never fights, not of your house). While roped they try to break free about every 30 s: keep guards near and use good rope. Animals are tamed at once. Rulers can't be captured.

Your house: name it in the Realm tab. Your people ("{Blackhide} Grak"), banners and realm carry the name.

Interact buttons: what a unit can do itself (fly, rest, dragon breath, mist, gem spells, its weapon's power) is on the Interact tab of its card, bottom left. What it can do with the creature beside it (feed from, breed with, rescue, capture, tame) is the Interact (I) button in the middle bar.

Altars and half-demons

Good Altar: Place tab, workshops. It takes stone, and the builder must also carry a lump of silver and a golden nugget. Pray at it for points; spend 150 on a Holy Shield; bless a Flask of Water into Holy Water.

Desecration: a Good Altar has a Sacrifice choice too. Each sacrifice darkens it; the third turns it into an Evil Altar, its worship the worth of what was given. Evil Altars can't be built any other way. There: sacrifice, Unholy Vigor (150 worship), bless a Flask of Blood into Unholy Blood, bless a flask of embers into Hellfire.

Flasks: a Metal Flask (Forge) or Leather Flask (Leatherworks). Fill it at water, from a pool of blood, or with embers beside an ember vent in the Ashlands. Double-click it in the bag for its choices, Drink among them.

Half-demon — possessed at the hell portal: red eyes, then red all over, then a Demon. Only those with no subrace can be taken: never the undead, vampires or devourers.

Marked — drank Unholy Blood. The dark gods' mark: smouldering eyes, small horns. +3 weapon damage and a little health back from every kill. Holy Water washes it out.

Ashen — drank Hellfire and lived through the burning (about half its health; a wounded unit dies). Grey as cinders, ember cracks under the skin. Can't burn, shrugs off heat, its blows may set foes alight; cold bites it twice as hard. Holy Water washes it out.

Hellblooded — born, not made: the child of a Demon and a half-demon of any kind, or of a Hellblooded parent, of the half-demon parent's race. Red-skinned, horned and tailed; a half-demon's strength, growing every level, and no fear of heat or cold. The NPC Alliance attacks it on sight, and nothing washes it out.

Silver and blessed weapons do every half-demon extra harm.

Fighting great beasts

A dragon or a great spider takes up the ground under its legs, head and wings: others walk round it or between its legs. Fight it part by part: select units and right-click the beast; its parts are laid out as they sit on its body, each with how whole it is, the one under the pointer lit. Or just attack it and each unit goes for the nearest part.

Legs: nothing is ever cut off a great beast, but a mangled leg makes it limp, and two leave it unable to walk until they mend. Head: the deadly blows. Wings: broken, it can't take off, and one in the air comes crashing down; they mend in a few minutes. Archers and thrown weapons at a beast in the air mostly hit its wings; a ballista is made for it: select it to see its reach and what it is aiming at.

With units selected, point at a beast: a ring marks the part under the pointer. Each part's health, and how long broken wings take to mend, are on its Health tab. Wild dragons fight back: they breathe fire on the thickest knot of your people, come down while hurt wings still carry them, and back off when badly hurt. A grown dragon (or a great spider queen) bites and breathes from two tiles away, where its head is, and steps back from anyone right under it.

Butchering a dead dragon gives, besides meat and bones, its scales (Dragonscale armour at the Forge: three pieces worn and fire can't take you), its great bones (laid over scales: Dragonbone armour at Bone crafting, the strongest), teeth and claws (Fang weapons at Bone crafting), its heart (Dragon's Blood: with a Strength Tonic and water, hot in the Cauldron, a Dragonheart Draught) and wing hide (serves as skin). A great spider gives chitin, fangs and a venom sac. Each beast has a light version at the Leatherworks (Dragonhide from wing hide, Spidersilk from silk set with chitin, Bonehide from hide studded with a dragon's bones) and a mage's robe (Dragonscale, Spider Silk and Dragonbone Robes: +10% to a school of magic).

Taming. A great beast is too big to knock out. Either steal an egg from a wild dragon's nest (pick it up; its mother hunts whoever carries it) and lay it down far from the nest to hatch into a dragon of your own; or chain it down: make Iron Chains at the Forge (2 iron ingots), right-click the beast, Chain it down. Your units wear it down without killing it, then bind it (a dragon takes 3 chains, a great spider 2). Then right-click it, Feed it raw meat, three times a minute or more apart, and it is yours; left unfed for three minutes it snaps a chain, and with none left it breaks free. Its card shows the chains left, how far it trusts you and how long until it is hungry; a young dragon's card, how long until it is full grown.

Lairs. A few cave mouths are a dragon's lair (scorched ground and bones before the mouth) or a spider's nest (webbed over): the grown beast sleeps inside on its hoard, with its eggs, and its young roam the cave. With units selected, right-click any cave mouth → Enter the cave: they walk there and go down together, up to 8. Kill the beast and carry the hoard out: a cleared lair stays cleared, and what you leave behind is still there next time. One visit per cave a day. A dragon's lair is floored with its gold. Your own dragon, grown and standing beside rock, can make its lair there (its cave box, or right-click it): your own cave with no visit limit, where treasure you leave stays, the gold floor grows with the hoard, and the dragon heals faster asleep on it.

Weapons and swing speed

Each weapon swings on its own time, whatever its wielder is doing; slower weapons hit harder, so a fight lasts about as long whatever is in hand.

Weapon moves. Every weapon has moves, and enchanted or rare ones also carry spells. Which a unit has depends on how its skills are built: each move needs the weapon's own skill (Sword, Axe, Bow...) and character skills (Attack, Strength, Defence, Ranged, Magic) at a level. A swordsman trained in Attack lunges, one trained in Strength swings a heavy blow, one trained in Defence ripostes. They're on the unit card's Spells tab, with its spells; locked ones say what they need. A unit uses its ready moves by itself in a fight (click Auto to keep one for when you choose), or click one to use it now. A move's blow always lands.

Enchanted weapons hold their own mana, shown on the Spells tab with their spells: their own powers, open to whoever wields them, and branch spells the wielder's skills unlock. Spells and powers cost the weapon's mana, never the unit's. It fills slowly by itself, faster from a Rune Stone carried in the bag (cut from a gem at the Gem cutter), and fastest standing beside a rune circuit with mana. A Crystal Staff tops up the other magic weapons its bearer carries.

Looking after your people

Food. Villagers eat from Food pots (1 stone) and Food stalls (1 wood), both in the Place tab's workshops; with none, they eat from the stockpile. Food never rots in them, and they count as food in your stock. Wood under a pot stews raw food hot (Add wood, Light), and "hot stew" is the best meal. At war, an enemy's food can be poisoned: a unit beside a pot with a poison in its bag gets "Slip in" buttons. Taste, Inspect and Empty are in the food box; an Antidote or a Doctor's purge cures the poisoned.

The Doctor. Build a Doctor's stall (1 wood) and give someone the Doctor job. Three herbs, Athelas, Feverfew and Milk thistle, grow wild around your village and grow back after picking. The Doctor makes remedies from them and treats whoever is worst off: bleeding, poisoned, feverish or badly hurt. With no herb to hand it posts a Gather order on the Board. A remedy in a bag is used like a potion, or by the bag rule.

Sickness. Food kept for an hour turns rotten and gives food poisoning; salt (from mined rock) keeps it. Rot, bad air from bodies, plague and sick neighbours can give the fever (coughing, fever, very ill). A Feverfew tonic cures it and gives a while of immunity. Wine: a few cups make a villager merry, then staggering, sick, and at last asleep; at the Tavern (1 wood) friends who drink together may brawl, with blows that never kill, until a sober guard breaks it up.

The fallen. Build a Graveyard (1 stone) and the gravedigger carries your dead there and buries them, and clears blood and mess when there is nobody to bury. Build an Operating table (1 wood): the Doctor carries downed villagers to it and can fit a lost limb from a body, a carried limb or a made false limb (iron, wood, bone, gem, or automail). A graft from another race brings some of its strength, but may be rejected, and demon flesh can drive its wearer mad. Lizardmen, Vampires and Werewolves regrow limbs by themselves.

Souls and blood. A rare soul stone turns up in mountain rock; used beside a dead villager's body it takes their soul, and used beside a soulless gargoyle of yours it wakes it with their name, level and skills. Drinking Unholy Blood (blessed at an Evil Altar) turns one drinker in four into a Vampire.

Click a villager to see how they look at a glance (pale, coughing, drunk, bleeding, a false arm) under their name, and everything wrong with them on the Health tab.

Building

How it works. Pick a piece in the Build tab and paint it on the ground: drag for walls, floors, roads and track, click for single pieces. Each plan becomes a construction task that shows what it needs. Select units first to make them its workers, or give villagers the Builder job. They fetch the material from your stockpiles and build it, floors first and walls last; a building's door goes in last of all, so nobody is shut out.

Walls and the wall-walk. A Fortress wall painted two or three rows side by side becomes one wide wall. Every finished wall that is not part of a house gets a walk on top. Right-click a wall, Man the wall here: your units climb up and stand two to a tile, a front rank and a back rank. A move order onto a walk finds the stairs or ramp by itself. The side facing out gets battlements and the side facing your yard a low lip; a wall standing alone in the open has battlements only on the side away from your banner. Breach a wall and the walk above it falls, with anyone on it.

Getting up. Wall stairs (1 stone) go beside a wall with a walk. A Ramp up the wall runs three tiles along it (3 stone), or is cut into the inner row of a wall two tiles thick (free). A 3×3 block of wall becomes a round Tower. A wooden wall with a walk is a timber rampart: pointed logs outside, a plank walk with a hand rail inside.

Gates. Leave a one-tile gap between two walls and set a door in it (Gate): the walk carries on over it. Between wooden walls it is a timber gatehouse with a little roof; between stone walls a stone arch. Either has a torch each side outside. A Portcullis fills a gap of up to 3 tiles (or a tunnel mouth), and a Drawbridge spans a moat in front of a gate (up to 5 tiles across). Lay rune lines to them from a lever: each pull costs 3 mana, or nothing while a turning axle beside them drives them. A portcullis never drops on anyone.

Houses and forts. Walls round a room whose floor is mostly built make a house. So does a small walled space of 4 to 40 tiles with a door or an open doorway, whatever its floor: its walls get no walk, and it gets a roof. A fort therefore needs a bigger yard (more than 40 tiles inside), or no way in. A strip of bank between a wall and a moat is not a doorway.

Roofs. Houses get thatch over timber walls and slate over stone: a pitched roof on a rectangle, sloping on every side on other shapes. While you play, every roof on screen is see-through so you can look inside; zoom far out and they turn solid. Halls in a mountain, and anything underground, have the rock for a roof.

Roads and bridges. A Cobbled road (1 stone a tile) makes walkers about 40% faster, a Dirt track (free) about 15% faster. Roads go on open ground, not over water, built floors, fields or stairs; a track can be cobbled over. Bridges cross water and chasms; stone ones cross lava too.

Halls in mountains. In Halls, press Ceiling ↑ for a higher roof (3 to 6), then drag a hall of 4 by 4 or more onto open ground or into a mountain. It lays out the walls (where it is cut into rock, the rock is the wall), pillars or beams to hold the roof, beds, a table or a fire and a workshop, and digs a tunnel to its door. With the camera on a hall inside a mountain, the rock in front of it is cut away so you can see in.

Mine carts. In Rails, drag track (1 wood a tile), put a Cart stop beside the stockpile and another at the mine, then a Mine cart on the track. It runs between the stops by itself, 20 loads at a time, and miners drop what they dig at the mine stop when it is nearer than the stockpile. Track makes no one walk faster.

Power and lifts. In Power, a Water wheel on a bank beside water makes 10 power, a Windmill under open sky 6 (10 on a mountain top). Drag a line of Axles from it to the machines it drives: Bellows beside a forge, smelter or melter (4 power; the smith works twice as fast), a Rune dynamo touching a rune circuit (5; it charges the crystals), a Lift (6). A line that can't feed all its machines runs none of them. A lift goes one level up or down: up a cliff or onto a wall walk, or down a shaft to the tunnels. Its winch stands where you build it, beside an axle. Units ride a running lift like stairs and leave one without power alone.

Fishing

Fishing spots bubble on the shore. Select units and click a spot (or right-click → Fish here); Fisher workers find spots themselves. Make a Fishing Rod, Fishing Net or Lobster Cage at the Carpentry and a Harpoon at the Forge. Without a tool, only shallow water can be fished, by hand.

Skills and levels

Skills level like RuneScape: every action earns experience (a swing of the axe or pick 4 XP, a craft 15–30, a cooked meal 30, a shrimp 10, a shark 110) and the level follows RuneScape's table: level 2 at 83 XP, 10 at 1,154, 50 at 101,333, 92 at 6,517,253 (half of 99) and 99 at 13,034,431. Levels go on to 120. It is slow on purpose: keep your people at the work they are good at.

A fair start: the common races start at level 1 in everything. Crafting still works 45% of the time at level 1 (certain from level 19), and chopping and mining work as if at level 12.

Rare races (Dragon, Spider, Devourer, Svartalfar, Illithid) only appear in the race list when drawn: 4% each, plus 3% for every achievement you have ever reached. Early raids on Normal are small and poorly trained until your settlement grows.
